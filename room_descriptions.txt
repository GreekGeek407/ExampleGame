Living Room
The small living room contains a couch, coffee table, and two 
armchairs. Pictures of various cousins adorn the walls along 
with two of your aunt's wretched attempts at oil painting. You 
can't help but notice that the carpet could use a good 
vacuuming. 

Doorways lead west to your father's office, north to your 
bedroom, and east to the kitchen. 
#
Bedroom
This is your domain and has been since your family moved here 
when you were six. A nightstand with a Superman lamp sits next 
to your twin bed. Posters of various anime characters cover one 
wall. A pile of laundry, slowly but surely attaining sentience, 
occupies the far corner. 

The door to the attic is in the ceiling above the pile of 
clothes. You can return south to the living room. 
#
Office
The office is cleaner than the rest of the house. The room is mostly 
tidy save for a growing pile of papers and files on the desk, which has 
a comfortable-looking chair in front of it. A laptop sits open, displaying 
a spreadsheet of frightening proportions. A window lets sunlight filter 
in, bringing life to the otherwise bland decor. 

The door on the east wall leads back to the living room. 
#
Kitchen
Your kitchen has seen a lot of use over the years. Nothing in here is 
new, but nothing is so neglected that it needs replacing. The faint 
scent of garlic and onions lingers in the air from last night's dinner. 
While the counter is a bit weathered, everything is sparkling clean. A 
block on the counter holds every kind of knife you could possibly need. 

The doorway to the west leads to the living room, and a rickety 
wooden door leads south to the garage. 
#
Garage
The delicious smells of the kitchen give way to the aroma of industry. 
Your dad's car sits on the stone floor at the center of the garage. The 
edges of the room are populated by tables covered in wrenches, nuts, 
bolts, and many instruments you have no idea how to use. Despite the 
apparent mess, you're certain that your dad would be able to tell if 
you moved anything. 

The door at the back of the garage leads north back into the kitchen. 
#
Attic
The attic is just over three feet tall at its tallest point, forcing 
you into an awkward squat position. The space is filled with various 
trinkets and discarded clothes, many of which have a thick layer of 
dust coating them. You spot a box of VHS tapes, several yellowed photo 
albums, and some long-forgotten holiday decorations, among other things. 

You can climb back down to your bedroom through the trapdoor in the floor.
